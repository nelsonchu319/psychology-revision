Key Notes

Textbook concepts from OpenStax Psychology 2e (paraphrased). Genes, neurons, systems, brain, and hormones that appear in the book belong here.

3.1 Human Genetics §3.1, ≈pp. 72–78

  • Evolution by natural selection frames why some genetic variants persist; evolutionary psychology asks about adaptive functions, while behavioral genetics estimates genetic/environmental contributions to individual differences.
  • Genes/alleles: dominant alleles show their phenotype when present; recessive phenotypes require homozygous recessive genotype.
  • Genotype = genetic makeup; phenotype = observable characteristics from genes × environment.
  • Range of reaction: genes set potential boundaries; environment influences where the phenotype falls within that range.
  • Epigenetics: same genotype can be expressed differently over time and contexts (including twin discussions in the text).

3.2 Cells of the Nervous System §3.2, ≈pp. 78–84

  • Neuron: dendrites (receive) → soma → axon → terminals; glia support/insulate (myelin).
  • Resting potential → threshold of excitation → action potential (all-or-none); myelin speeds conduction (damage in MS).
  • Synapse: neurotransmitters cross the cleft; reuptake clears transmitter; agonists mimic/enhance, antagonists block (e.g., SSRI logic; dopamine pathways and Parkinson’s treatments).

3.3 Parts of the Nervous System §3.3, ≈pp. 84–86

  • CNS = brain + spinal cord; PNS = somatic + autonomic.
  • Somatic: sensory input and voluntary motor output.
  • Autonomic: sympathetic (fight-or-flight) vs parasympathetic (rest-and-digest).

3.4 The Brain and Spinal Cord §3.4, ≈pp. 86–96

  • Spinal cord supports reflexes; hindbrain–midbrain–forebrain organization; cortex folded into gyri/sulci.
  • Lobes: frontal (motor, executive; Broca’s area for speech production), parietal (somatosensory), temporal (auditory; Wernicke’s for comprehension), occipital (vision).
  • Corpus callosum / split-brain insights into lateralization.
  • Subcortical: thalamus (sensory relay), limbic structures (hippocampus, amygdala, hypothalamus), midbrain (e.g., substantia nigra & VTA; Parkinson’s / reward).
  • Imaging overview: CT, PET, MRI/fMRI, EEG—different tools for structure vs activity; choose the tool that matches the question.

3.5 The Endocrine System §3.5, ≈pp. 96–100

  • Hormones travel in blood—typically slower/broader than synaptic transmission.
  • Pituitary as master gland; thyroid (growth/metabolism); adrenal involvement in stress; hypothalamus as nervous–endocrine interface.

Further Theoretical Depth

External neuroscience and behavior-genetics sources that extend beyond the textbook’s sketches of plasticity, heritability, and stress biology.

Hebb proposed that lasting memory depends on activity-dependent synaptic change—“cells that fire together wire together”—a theoretical bridge from psychology of learning to synaptic physiology long before modern LTP labs.

Hebb, D. O. (1949). The Organization of Behavior: A Neuropsychological Theory. Wiley.

這是學習／記憶的古典神經理論，不是 OpenStax 章節的複述。

Kandel’s Aplysia program showed that simple forms of learning alter synaptic strength and gene expression in identified circuits—empirical grounding for plasticity as a cellular mechanism of memory.

Kandel, E. R. (2001). The molecular biology of memory storage: A dialogue between genes and synapses. Science, 294(5544), 1030–1038. (Nobel-related program summarized in that review.)

Behavior-genetic work (twin, adoption, and molecular designs) estimates that many psychological traits show substantial heritability and substantial nonshared-environment influence—so “genes matter” never meant “environment is irrelevant.”

Plomin, R., DeFries, J. C., Knopik, V. S., & Neiderhiser, J. M. (2016). Top 10 replicated findings from behavioral genetics. Perspectives on Psychological Science, 11(1), 3–23. (Accessible overview of classic twin/adoption logic.)

Damasio’s somatic-marker hypothesis argues that bodily/affective signals guide decision-making under uncertainty—linking emotion systems to “rational” choice, beyond a simple lobe-function list.

Damasio, A. R. (1994). Descartes’ Error: Emotion, Reason, and the Human Brain. Putnam. See also Bechara, A., Damasio, H., Tranel, D., & Damasio, A. R. (1997). Deciding advantageously before knowing the advantageous strategy. Science, 275(5304), 1293–1295.

Sapolsky’s stress biology emphasizes chronic HPA-axis activation (glucocorticoids) as a pathway from prolonged threat to bodily wear—useful when connecting textbook adrenal/stress notes to allostatic-load thinking.

Sapolsky, R. M. (2004). Why Zebras Don’t Get Ulcers (3rd ed.). Holt. (Popular synthesis grounded in his stress endocrinology research program.)
