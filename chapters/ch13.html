Key Notes

Textbook concepts from OpenStax Psychology 2e (paraphrased).

13.1 What Is I-O Psychology? §13.1, ≈pp. 446–453

  • I-O psychology applies psychological principles to the workplace: industrial (job analysis, selection, training, appraisal) and organizational (social systems, motivation, culture).
  • Historical roots include early selection testing and the Hawthorne studies’ attention to social factors.

13.2 Industrial Psychology §13.2, ≈pp. 454–463

  • Job analysis (task- vs worker-oriented); candidate testing & interviews; training; performance appraisal.
  • Legal constraints on discriminatory hiring; bias risks in selection tools.

13.3 Organizational Psychology §13.3, ≈pp. 464–473

  • Job satisfaction predictors/outcomes; leadership theories (e.g., Theory X/Y, transactional vs transformational themes as in the chapter).
  • Organizational culture/climate; diversity; harassment (hostile environment; quid pro quo); work–family conflict; telecommuting themes.

中文補充：Industrial 偏「選人／評核／訓練」；Organizational 偏「動機／領導／文化／壓力」。

13.4 Human Factors §13.4, ≈pp. 474–482

  • Human factors / ergonomics: design tools, machines, and work environments to fit human capabilities and reduce error/strain.

Further Theoretical Depth

External journals and classic books beyond the textbook sketch.

The Hawthorne studies are often mythologized; modern readings stress careful interpretation of attention, norms, and methodology.

Mayo, E. (1933). The human problems of an industrial civilization. Macmillan. archive.org. Critical review: Olson, R., Verley, J., Santos, L., & Salas, C. (2004). What we teach students about the Hawthorne studies. The Industrial-Organizational Psychologist. SIOP PDF

Hackman and Oldham’s job characteristics model links core job dimensions to critical psychological states and outcomes.

Hackman, J. R., & Oldham, G. R. (1976). Motivation through the design of work: Test of a theory. Organizational Behavior and Human Performance, 16(2), 250–279. doi.org/10.1016/0030-5073(76)90016-7

Bass’s transformational leadership research distinguishes inspirational change-oriented leadership from transactional exchange.

Bass, B. M. (1990). From transactional to transformational leadership: Learning to share the vision. Organizational Dynamics, 18(3), 19–31. doi.org/10.1016/0090-2616(90)90061-S

Schmidt and Hunter’s meta-analytic work is a classic on the validity of selection procedures.

Schmidt, F. L., & Hunter, J. E. (1998). The validity and utility of selection methods in personnel psychology. Psychological Bulletin, 124(2), 262–274. doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.124.2.262
