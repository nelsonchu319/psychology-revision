Key Notes

Textbook concepts from OpenStax Psychology 2e (paraphrased).

12.1 What Is Social Psychology? §12.1, ≈pp. 400–405

  • Social psychology: how people affect one another’s thoughts, feelings, and behaviors; situationism vs dispositionism.
  • Fundamental attribution error; actor–observer bias; self-serving bias; just-world hypothesis.

12.2 Self-presentation §12.2, ≈pp. 406–407

  • Social roles, social norms, scripts; Stanford prison experiment as a dramatic (and debated) illustration of roles/power.

12.3 Attitudes and Persuasion §12.3, ≈pp. 408–413

  • Attitudes: affective, behavioral, cognitive components; cognitive dissonance (Festinger); persuasion routes / features of speaker-message-audience.

12.4 Conformity, Compliance, Obedience §12.4, ≈pp. 414–421

  • Asch conformity; compliance techniques; Milgram obedience; groupthink; group polarization; social facilitation/loafing.

中文補充：從眾（Asch）≠服從（Milgram）≠合規技巧；分清規範影響與權威指令。

12.5–12.7 Prejudice, Aggression, Prosocial §12.5–12.7, ≈pp. 422–444

  • Prejudice, stereotypes, discrimination; self-fulfilling prophecy; in-group/out-group; scapegoating.
  • Aggression: hostile vs instrumental; bullying; bystander themes; altruism; empathy; homophily; Sternberg’s triangular theory of love; social exchange / reciprocity.

Further Theoretical Depth

External journals and classic books beyond the textbook sketch.

Festinger’s dissonance theory predicts attitude change when behavior and belief clash without sufficient external justification.

Festinger, L., & Carlsmith, J. M. (1959). Cognitive consequences of forced compliance. Journal of Abnormal and Social Psychology, 58(2), 203–210. doi.org/10.1037/h0041593

Asch’s line studies remain the standard demonstration of normative conformity pressure.

Asch, S. E. (1956). Studies of independence and conformity: I. A minority of one against a unanimous majority. Psychological Monographs, 70(9), 1–70. doi.org/10.1037/h0093718

Milgram’s obedience paradigm shows destructive compliance to legitimate-seeming authority—ethics and interpretation still discussed.

Milgram, S. (1963). Behavioral study of obedience. Journal of Abnormal and Social Psychology, 67(4), 371–378. doi.org/10.1037/h0040525

Darley and Latané’s bystander work links diffusion of responsibility to helping failures in groups.

Darley, J. M., & Latané, B. (1968). Bystander intervention in emergencies: Diffusion of responsibility. Journal of Personality and Social Psychology, 8(4), 377–383. doi.org/10.1037/h0025589

Must-know contrasts

High-yield comparisons for this chapter. Focus on common confusions and everyday examples. Paraphrased from OpenStax Psychology 2e ideas plus named classics; not verbatim textbook text.

Must-know contrast table

Dispositional vs situational attribution

ConceptCore ideaCommon confusionEveryday example
Dispositional attributionExplain behavior via traitsJumping to “they’re lazy/rude” for others’ behavior (fundamental attribution error pattern)A classmate arrives late and you immediately think “lazy,” ignoring the typhoon MTR delay
Situational attributionExplain via context/pressuresUnderusing situation for strangers while overusing it to excuse ourselves (actor–observer pattern)You notice the signal fault SMS and attribute their lateness to the subway, not character

Conformity vs obedience

ConceptCore ideaCommon confusionEveryday example
ConformityMatch group norm/pressure (Asch-type)Using conformity and obedience as synonyms on examsYou laugh at a weak joke because the whole tutorial group laughed first
ObedienceFollow a direct authority order (Milgram-type)Calling peer fashion pressure “obedience” when no authority gave an orderAn intern follows a manager’s written order to alter a report despite feeling it’s wrong

Prejudice vs discrimination

ConceptCore ideaCommon confusionEveryday example
PrejudiceAttitude / affective biasAssuming that if hiring looks fair, prejudice cannot exist (attitude ≠ always overt act)Someone privately feels cold distrust toward a group while smiling politely in class
DiscriminationBiased behavior / treatmentAssuming prejudice always shows as loud, overt discrimination you can filmTwo equal CVs; only the one with a majority-sounding name gets the interview invite

Theory vs evidence

Theory / claimSupporting evidenceLimits / challenges
Asch: people often conform to clear majority errorLine-judgment studies; conformity drops with a dissenter allyCulture, task difficulty, and anonymity moderate rates; not everyone conforms
Milgram: many obey authority to harm a learner in lab setupHigh obedience rates under experimenter pressure; situational levers matterEthics critique; generalizing to all real atrocities needs care; agency still present
Cognitive dissonance: inconsistent cognitions motivate attitude/behavior change (Festinger)Insufficient-justification and induced-compliance effects classic in social psychSelf-perception theory offers alternatives; cultural variation in dissonance patterns
