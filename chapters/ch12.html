Key Notes

Textbook concepts from OpenStax Psychology 2e (paraphrased).

12.1 What Is Social Psychology? §12.1, ≈pp. 400–405

  • Social psychology: how people affect one another’s thoughts, feelings, and behaviors; situationism vs dispositionism.
  • Fundamental attribution error; actor–observer bias; self-serving bias; just-world hypothesis.

12.2 Self-presentation §12.2, ≈pp. 406–407

  • Social roles, social norms, scripts; Stanford prison experiment as a dramatic (and debated) illustration of roles/power.

12.3 Attitudes and Persuasion §12.3, ≈pp. 408–413

  • Attitudes: affective, behavioral, cognitive components; cognitive dissonance (Festinger); persuasion routes / features of speaker-message-audience.

12.4 Conformity, Compliance, Obedience §12.4, ≈pp. 414–421

  • Asch conformity; compliance techniques; Milgram obedience; groupthink; group polarization; social facilitation/loafing.

中文補充：從眾（Asch）≠服從（Milgram）≠合規技巧；分清規範影響與權威指令。

12.5–12.7 Prejudice, Aggression, Prosocial §12.5–12.7, ≈pp. 422–444

  • Prejudice, stereotypes, discrimination; self-fulfilling prophecy; in-group/out-group; scapegoating.
  • Aggression: hostile vs instrumental; bullying; bystander themes; altruism; empathy; homophily; Sternberg’s triangular theory of love; social exchange / reciprocity.

Further Theoretical Depth

External journals and classic books beyond the textbook sketch.

Festinger’s dissonance theory predicts attitude change when behavior and belief clash without sufficient external justification.

Festinger, L., & Carlsmith, J. M. (1959). Cognitive consequences of forced compliance. Journal of Abnormal and Social Psychology, 58(2), 203–210. doi.org/10.1037/h0041593

Asch’s line studies remain the standard demonstration of normative conformity pressure.

Asch, S. E. (1956). Studies of independence and conformity: I. A minority of one against a unanimous majority. Psychological Monographs, 70(9), 1–70. doi.org/10.1037/h0093718

Milgram’s obedience paradigm shows destructive compliance to legitimate-seeming authority—ethics and interpretation still discussed.

Milgram, S. (1963). Behavioral study of obedience. Journal of Abnormal and Social Psychology, 67(4), 371–378. doi.org/10.1037/h0040525

Darley and Latané’s bystander work links diffusion of responsibility to helping failures in groups.

Darley, J. M., & Latané, B. (1968). Bystander intervention in emergencies: Diffusion of responsibility. Journal of Personality and Social Psychology, 8(4), 377–383. doi.org/10.1037/h0025589
