Key Notes

Textbook concepts from OpenStax Psychology 2e (paraphrased).

6.1 What Is Learning? §6.1, ≈pp. 181–183

  • Instincts and reflexes are innate; learning is a relatively permanent change in behavior or knowledge from experience.
  • Three major types: classical conditioning, operant conditioning, observational learning.
  • Classical and operant are forms of associative learning.

6.2 Classical Conditioning §6.2, ≈pp. 183–191

  • Pavlov: associate events that repeatedly occur together; map a reflexive response onto a new stimulus.
  • Terms: UCS, UCR, NS→CS, CR; acquisition, extinction, spontaneous recovery, generalization, discrimination.
  • Watson / Little Albert: conditioned fear in an infant—classical conditioning and some fears.
  • Higher-order conditioning and real-world applications (e.g., advertising, taste aversion themes in the chapter).

中文補充：古典制約重點是「刺激—刺激」聯結，反應原本是反射性的。

6.3 Operant Conditioning §6.3, ≈pp. 192–202

  • Skinner: consequences after a behavior change the likelihood of that behavior.
  • All reinforcement (positive or negative) increases behavior; all punishment decreases it.
  • Positive = add stimulus; negative = remove stimulus.
  • Shaping; primary vs secondary reinforcers; continuous vs partial schedules (fixed/variable × ratio/interval)—partial often more extinction-resistant.

6.4 Observational Learning (Modeling) §6.4, ≈pp. 203–206

  • Bandura: learn by watching others, then modeling.
  • Steps: attention, retention, reproduction, motivation.
  • Vicarious reinforcement / punishment: seeing the model rewarded or punished changes imitation likelihood.
  • Classic Bobo doll demonstrations of modeled aggression.

Further Theoretical Depth

External journals and classic books beyond the textbook sketch.

Garcia and Koelling’s work on taste aversion showed biological constraints: not all CS–UCS pairings are equally learnable—prepared associations matter.

Garcia, J., & Koelling, R. A. (1966). Relation of cue to consequence in avoidance learning. Psychonomic Science, 4, 123–124. doi.org/10.3758/BF03342209

Rescorla emphasized that contingency (predictiveness), not mere contiguity, is central to what is learned in classical conditioning.

Rescorla, R. A. (1988). Pavlovian conditioning: It’s not what you think it is. American Psychologist, 43(3), 151–160. doi.org/10.1037/0003-066X.43.3.151

Bandura’s social-cognitive framing treats observational learning as active cognitive modeling—not a thin copy of Skinnerian shaping alone.

Bandura, A. (1977). Social Learning Theory. Prentice Hall. Google Books. See also Bandura, A., Ross, D., & Ross, S. A. (1961). Transmission of aggression through imitation of aggressive models. Journal of Abnormal and Social Psychology, 63, 575–582. doi.org/10.1037/h0045925

Bobo doll 與社會學習理論的完整論述在專書／原論文，比課本摘要更深。

Tolman’s latent learning / cognitive maps challenged strict S–R accounts by showing learning without immediate reinforcement.

Tolman, E. C. (1948). Cognitive maps in rats and men. Psychological Review, 55(4), 189–208. doi.org/10.1037/h0061626
