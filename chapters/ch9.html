Key Notes

Textbook concepts from OpenStax Psychology 2e (paraphrased).

9.1 What Is Lifespan Development? §9.1, ≈pp. 280–283

  • Development across the lifespan in physical, cognitive, and psychosocial domains.
  • Normative approach; issues: continuity vs discontinuity; one course vs many; nature vs nurture.

9.2 Lifespan Theories §9.2, ≈pp. 284–291

  • Freud’s psychosexual stages (historical); Erikson’s psychosocial crises across eight stages.
  • Piaget: sensorimotor → preoperational → concrete operational → formal operational (schemas, assimilation/accommodation, object permanence, conservation, egocentrism).
  • Kohlberg’s moral levels (preconventional, conventional, postconventional); Carol Gilligan’s care-perspective critique is often paired in discussion.

中文補充：皮亞傑重「怎麼想」、Erikson 重「社會任務／自我」、Kohlberg 重「道德推理階段」。

9.3 Stages of Development §9.3, ≈pp. 292–311

  • Prenatal: germinal, embryonic, fetal; teratogens; newborn reflexes; attachment (Bowlby/Ainsworth themes); parenting styles.
  • Childhood/adolescence: cognitive & social growth, puberty, identity; emerging adulthood themes as in the chapter.
  • Adulthood: early/middle/late—relationships, work, cognitive changes, socioemotional selectivity themes.

9.4 Death and Dying §9.4, ≈pp. 312–320

  • Kübler-Ross stages as a widely cited model (denial, anger, bargaining, depression, acceptance)—not a rigid universal sequence.
  • Hospice / palliative care goals emphasizing comfort and dignity.

Further Theoretical Depth

External journals and classic books beyond the textbook sketch.

Piaget’s stage theory remains the backbone of cognitive-development questions even where later research qualifies ages and cultural generality.

Piaget, J. (1952). The origins of intelligence in children (M. Cook, Trans.). International Universities Press. archive.org

Erikson’s psychosocial stages extend development into adulthood with identity, intimacy, generativity, and integrity crises.

Erikson, E. H. (1950). Childhood and society. Norton. W. W. Norton

Ainsworth’s Strange Situation operationalized attachment patterns that students must map to caregiver sensitivity.

Ainsworth, M. D. S., Blehar, M. C., Waters, E., & Wall, S. (1978). Patterns of attachment. Erlbaum. Routledge (reissue). Classic article: Ainsworth, M. D. S., & Bell, S. M. (1970). Attachment, exploration, and separation. Child Development, 41(1), 49–67. doi.org/10.2307/1127388

Vygotsky’s sociocultural emphasis (ZPD, scaffolding) complements Piaget’s stage focus with social mediation of cognition.

Vygotsky, L. S. (1978). Mind in society (M. Cole et al., Eds.). Harvard University Press. Harvard University Press
