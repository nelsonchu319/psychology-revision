  • From supernatural treatments/asylums to Pinel & Dix reforms; deinstitutionalization; today’s short-stay hospitals + outpatient care by diverse professionals.

16.2 Types of Treatment §16.2, ≈pp. 602–615

  • Psychotherapy vs biomedical therapy.
  • Psychoanalysis/psychodynamic (free association, dream analysis, transference); play therapy; behavior therapy (counterconditioning, exposure, systematic desensitization, aversive conditioning, token economies).
  • Cognitive & CBT; humanistic (Rogers client-centered: active listening, UPR, genuineness, empathy).
  • Biomedical: psychotropic meds classes as surveyed; ECT; newer brain-stimulation themes as in the chapter.

Further Theoretical Depth

External journals and classic books beyond the textbook sketch.

Wolpe’s systematic desensitization is the classic counterconditioning package for phobias.

Wolpe, J. (1958). Psychotherapy by reciprocal inhibition. Stanford University Press. Google Books. Related: Wolpe, J. (1961). The systematic desensitization treatment of neuroses. Journal of Nervous and Mental Disease, 132, 189–203. doi.org/10.1097/00005053-196103000-00001

Beck’s CBT manualized approach targets automatic thoughts, cognitive distortions, and behavioral experiments.

Beck, A. T. (1979). Cognitive therapy of depression. Guilford Press. archive.org

Rogers’s necessary-and-sufficient conditions paper is the humanistic therapy cornerstone.

Rogers, C. R. (1957). The necessary and sufficient conditions of therapeutic personality change. Journal of Consulting Psychology, 21(2), 95–103. doi.org/10.1037/h0045357

Smith and Glass’s early meta-analysis helped establish psychotherapy’s average effectiveness against controls.

Smith, M. L., & Glass, G. V. (1977). Meta-analysis of psychotherapy outcome studies. American Psychologist, 32(9), 752–760. doi.org/10.1037/0003-066X.32.9.752

Must-know contrasts

High-yield comparisons for this chapter. Focus on common confusions and everyday examples. Paraphrased from OpenStax Psychology 2e ideas plus named classics; not verbatim textbook text.

Must-know contrast table

Biomedical treatment vs psychotherapy

ConceptCore ideaCommon confusionEveryday example
BiomedicalMedication, ECT, other biological interventionsAssuming a prescription replaces all need for skills practice or psychotherapyAn SSRI is prescribed for major depression while CBT skills are still on the table
PsychotherapyTalk / behavioral interventions with a clinicianAssuming talk therapy alone is always enough for every severe disorderWeekly CBT sessions with between-session thought records and exposure homework

CBT vs psychodynamic therapy

ConceptCore ideaCommon confusionEveryday example
CBTChange thoughts/behaviors maintaining problems; structured / present-focusedClaiming CBT “ignores emotion” because it also targets thoughts and behaviorsBefore a presentation you log the thought “I’ll fail publicly” and rate the anxiety 0–100
PsychodynamicExplore unconscious patterns, insight, relationship themesThinking psychodynamic therapy means only classic Freudian couches and dream booksIn therapy you link repeated people-pleasing at work to early caregiver patterns

Efficacy vs effectiveness

ConceptCore ideaCommon confusionEveryday example
EfficacyWorks under controlled trial conditionsTreating one famous RCT as proof the treatment works identically in every busy clinicA university trial with strict inclusion criteria and trained research therapists
EffectivenessWorks in real-world routine careIgnoring that comorbid, messy community clinics differ from screened lab samplesThe same protocol’s outcomes in a busy public community clinic with mixed caseloads

Theory vs evidence

Theory / claimSupporting evidenceLimits / challenges
CBT is efficacious for many anxiety and depressive disordersLarge RCT/meta-analytic literature; homework and exposure components matter (OpenStax Ch 16)Not universally best; engagement, comorbidities, and therapist skill vary outcomes
Common factors (alliance, expectation) explain shared therapy benefitsAlliance predicts outcome across modalities; placebo/expectation effects documentedDoesn’t mean techniques are irrelevant; specific factors matter for some problems (e.g., exposure for phobia)
Biomedical treatments can reduce symptoms for severe disordersAntidepressant/antipsychotic evidence bases; ECT for some treatment-resistant depression casesSide effects, adherence, relapse risk; combining with psychosocial care often optimal
