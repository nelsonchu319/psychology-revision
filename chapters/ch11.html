Key Notes

Textbook concepts from OpenStax Psychology 2e (paraphrased).

11.1 What Is Personality? §11.1, ≈pp. 360–361

  • Personality: long-standing traits and patterns that propel individuals to think, feel, and behave in similar ways across situations.

11.2–11.3 Psychodynamic & Neo-Freudians §11.2–11.3, ≈pp. 362–371

  • Freud: id/ego/superego; conscious–preconscious–unconscious; defense mechanisms; psychosexual stages.
  • Neo-Freudians: Adler (inferiority/individual psychology), Erikson, Jung (collective unconscious/archetypes), Horney (coping styles; critique of Freud’s views on women).

11.4–11.5 Learning & Humanistic §11.4–11.5, ≈pp. 372–377

  • Behavioral: personality as learned response patterns; Bandura reciprocal determinism & self-efficacy; Julian Rotter locus of control.
  • Humanistic: Maslow; Rogers—self-concept, ideal vs real self, unconditional positive regard.

中文補充：行為／社會認知重「學得與信念」，人本主義重「成長與一致性」。

11.6–11.7 Biological & Traits §11.6–11.7, ≈pp. 378–383

  • Heritability / temperament; Eysenck dimensions; Big Five (OCEAN): openness, conscientiousness, extraversion, agreeableness, neuroticism.
  • Trait vs situation debate (Mischel) as framed in the chapter.

11.8–11.9 Culture & Assessment §11.8–11.9, ≈pp. 384–398

  • Individualist vs collectivist contexts shaping personality expression.
  • Self-report inventories (MMPI, MBTI caveats as discussed) vs projective tests (Rorschach, TAT)—reliability/validity issues.

Further Theoretical Depth

External journals and classic books beyond the textbook sketch.

The Five-Factor Model is the dominant trait taxonomy in contemporary personality research.

McCrae, R. R., & Costa, P. T., Jr. (1987). Validation of the five-factor model of personality across instruments and observers. Journal of Personality and Social Psychology, 52(1), 81–90. doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.52.1.81

Mischel’s critique sparked the person–situation debate and later social-cognitive reconceptualizations of consistency.

Mischel, W. (1968). Personality and assessment. Wiley. Google Books. See also Mischel, W., & Shoda, Y. (1995). A cognitive-affective system theory of personality. Psychological Review, 102(2), 246–268. doi.org/10.1037/0033-295X.102.2.246

Rogers’s client-centered conditions (empathy, congruence, UPR) link personality growth to therapeutic climate.

Rogers, C. R. (1957). The necessary and sufficient conditions of therapeutic personality change. Journal of Consulting Psychology, 21(2), 95–103. doi.org/10.1037/h0045357

Bandura’s reciprocal determinism ties traits-in-context to learning without abandoning cognition.

Bandura, A. (1978). The self system in reciprocal determinism. American Psychologist, 33(4), 344–358. doi.org/10.1037/0003-066X.33.4.344
